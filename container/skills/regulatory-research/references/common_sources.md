# Common Regulatory Sources

This reference document provides links to commonly-used official sources for state statutes, administrative regulations, and local ordinances. Always verify these are the most current official sources before relying on them.

## General Guidance

**Always prioritize:**
1. Official state/local government websites
2. Secretary of State or comparable regulatory authority
3. Agency websites for guidance documents

**Verify:**
- The source is the official, authoritative version
- The version is current (check effective date)
- No recent amendments have superseded the version being reviewed

## Massachusetts Resources

### State Statutes
**Massachusetts General Laws (MGL)**
- **Official Source:** https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws
- **Maintained by:** Massachusetts Legislature
- **Notes:** Browse by chapter or search by keyword. Each chapter covers a different subject area (e.g., Chapter 94G is the Cannabis Control Act)

### State Regulations  
**Code of Massachusetts Regulations (CMR)**
- **Official Source:** https://www.mass.gov/regulations
- **Maintained by:** Secretary of the Commonwealth
- **Notes:** Organized by agency code (e.g., 935 CMR is Cannabis Control Commission regulations). Use the search function or browse by agency.

### Cannabis-Specific Resources
**Cannabis Control Commission**
- **Website:** https://masscannabiscontrol.com/
- **Regulations:** 935 CMR 500.000 (Adult Use), 935 CMR 501.000 (Medical Use)
- **Guidance:** https://masscannabiscontrol.com/regulations-guidance/
- **Notes:** The CCC website provides additional guidance documents, advisory bulletins, and FAQs that supplement the regulations

### Local Ordinances and Zoning Codes

Municipal codes are maintained by individual cities and towns. Common patterns:

**Direct municipal websites:**
- **Format:** [CityName].gov or [TownName].ma.us
- **Common locations:** Under "Government" → "Town Clerk" or "Zoning Board"
- **Example (Norton):** https://www.nortonma.org/ (navigate to zoning bylaws/ordinances)

**Third-party code repositories:**
- **General Code:** https://ecode360.com/ (many MA municipalities)
- **Municode:** https://library.municode.com/ (some MA municipalities)
- **Notes:** These are often the official versions by contract, but always verify with the municipality

**How to find a specific municipal code:**
1. Search: "[City/Town Name] MA zoning bylaws" or "[City/Town Name] MA general bylaws"
2. Check the municipality's official website first
3. If not available directly, check General Code or Municode
4. Verify effective date and any pending amendments

## Agency-Specific Resources

Many regulatory schemes have dedicated agency websites with additional interpretive guidance:

**Pattern for finding agency guidance:**
1. Identify the agency that promulgates/enforces the regulation
2. Visit the agency's official website
3. Look for sections labeled: "Guidance," "Interpretive Bulletins," "Advisory Opinions," "FAQs," "Technical Assistance"
4. Check for recent bulletins or updates that may affect interpretation

## Research Tips

**Using web_search effectively:**
- Search for the specific regulation number (e.g., "935 CMR 500.002")
- Include the jurisdiction (e.g., "Massachusetts marijuana regulations")
- Add "official" to find authoritative sources
- Check the date on search results to ensure currency

**Using web_fetch:**
- Always use web_fetch to retrieve full regulatory text rather than relying on snippets
- Fetch the specific section URL when possible
- If the regulation is in PDF format, fetch the PDF and extract text

**Verifying currency:**
- Check for "last updated" or "effective date" information
- Look for notices about recent amendments
- Cross-reference multiple sources if unsure
- When in doubt, check with the relevant agency

## Common Pitfalls

**Outdated cached versions:** Google and other search engines may show old cached versions of regulations. Always click through to the official source and verify the effective date.

**Unofficial annotated codes:** Some legal research sites (Westlaw, Lexis, Justia) provide annotated versions with case law. These are useful but may not reflect the most recent amendments. Always verify against the official unannotated version.

**Proposals vs. final regulations:** When researching recent regulations, be careful not to confuse proposed regulations with final, adopted regulations. Check the status carefully.

**Local code hosting:** Some municipalities don't maintain their codes online. If you cannot find a municipal code online, note this and recommend the user contact the town clerk or zoning board directly.
